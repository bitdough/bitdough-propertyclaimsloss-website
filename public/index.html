  4. Award signedAny two of the three sign, setting the amount of loss.

What is insurance appraisal?

Insurance appraisal is a policy-based form of alternative dispute resolution for property claims. It is used when the insured and the insurance company agree that a loss is covered but disagree on the amount of loss. Each side selects an appraiser, the appraisers select an umpire, and a written award signed by any two of the three determines the amount. It is typically faster and less expensive than litigation.

The process

From demand to award in five steps

Most residential and commercial property policies follow the same basic appraisal sequence. Your policy wording and state law control the details.

  1. Demand

    Either party makes a written demand for appraisal after a disagreement on the amount of loss.

  2. Select appraisers

    Each side names its own competent, disinterested appraiser, commonly within 20 days.

  3. Select umpire

    The appraisers agree on an umpire, commonly within 15 days, or ask a court to appoint one.

  4. Evaluate

    The appraisers inspect, compare estimates and resolve as many items as possible.

  5. Award

    Disputed items go to the umpire. An award signed by any two sets the amount of loss.

Quick answers

Common questions about appraisal

What is insurance appraisal?

Insurance appraisal is a dispute-resolution process written into most property insurance policies. When the policyholder and the insurer agree the loss is covered but disagree on how much it is worth, either side can demand appraisal. Each side hires its own appraiser, the two appraisers choose an umpire, and an agreement signed by any two of the three sets the amount of loss.

What is the difference between an appraiser and an umpire?

An appraiser is selected by one party, investigates the loss and works to reach an agreed amount with the other side's appraiser. An umpire is a neutral third person chosen by the two appraisers, or appointed by a court, who decides only the items the appraisers cannot agree on.

Does appraisal decide whether my claim is covered?

No. Appraisal decides the amount of loss. Questions about whether the policy covers a loss, or whether a policy condition was met, are coverage questions that are generally handled by the insurer, by negotiation, or in court.

Who can hire Property Claims Loss?

Policyholders, public adjusters, contractors, attorneys and insurance carriers retain us as appraisers. Appraisers on both sides, or a court, may select us as umpire.
